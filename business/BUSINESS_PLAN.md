# Business plan — Edwards Technology & Operations

## Executive concept
A small, owner-operated technology services agency helping small businesses improve websites, eliminate repetitive administrative work, and run reliable Linux/cloud infrastructure. Start with fixed-scope projects; add recurring support only after processes and service capacity are established.

## Ideal first customers
Local trades and service businesses, property management teams, boutique e-commerce merchants, and small professional offices that need simple technology improvements but lack internal IT staff.

## Initial offer
A **Business Technology Tune-Up**: discovery call, review of existing workflow/website, written recommendations, and one clearly defined paid implementation. Every client receives a written scope, estimated timeline, testing, and handover notes.

## Illustrative services (price only after scoping)
- Starter business website: $500–$1,500 project.
- Workflow automation: $300–$1,000 project.
- Server deployment/maintenance: custom project fee and $100–$500 monthly support if appropriate.
- Operational process review: $50–$100/hour.

These are planning assumptions, not researched local-market prices. Change them after customer interviews and cost analysis.

## Customer acquisition
1. Identify 20 local businesses with outdated websites or manual scheduling/reporting challenges.
2. Find the decision-maker and send a brief, personalized offer to diagnose one problem.
3. Conduct an introductory conversation before quoting work.
4. Send a written proposal; request a deposit where appropriate.
5. Deliver a small successful project, obtain consent for a case study, and seek referrals.

## Operations
- Track leads from the site's private inquiry dashboard.
- Store private proposals and client credentials outside the public GitHub repository.
- Use a separate account, contract, and project workspace for each customer.
- Define support hours, scope, responsibility, backup ownership, and response times before accepting managed-services contracts.

## 90-day launch roadmap
**Days 1–15:** choose/verify legal name; speak with 10 prospective customers; define one core offer; register and insure the business as applicable.
**Days 16–30:** customize and deploy website with domain, TLS, privacy notice, rate limiting and monitored backups; prepare sample work and proposal.
**Days 31–60:** conduct outreach; run discovery calls; complete a first paid project; refine pricing and scope.
**Days 61–90:** pursue repeat work and referrals; assess whether a support retainer is profitable and sustainable.

## Risks to manage
Customer acquisition, underpricing, support overload, handling customer data, scope creep, liability, and availability. Do not advertise 24/7 coverage unless it can be delivered. Do not accept regulated/sensitive data without appropriate legal and security controls.

## Success metrics
Qualified leads, discovery calls, proposals sent, proposals accepted, project gross margin, cash collected, repeat engagements, and customer referrals.
